History, not rules: `internals/snapshots.md` wins where they differ.

- The design was settled after nine review rounds (2026-10-25), modelled on Platform.sh's backups: one consistent moment of everything with data, binary, and a restore that brings the code back unless told otherwise (`--no-code`).
- Why everything with data and no `custom` script: a snapshot is the copy that must be complete whatever the project's scripts do; a service's copy label says whose job a sync's copy is, never whether a backup may skip it.
- A take once accepted a service with data whose only volume was anonymous (its data copied by its scripts in syncs): the snapshot held nothing of it and restored as complete (review). Hence the refusal of a data service with no entry or named volume left.
- Settling a killed sync holder's files before a take came from a review of 2026-10-25: a complete restore clears marks, so a snapshot taken over half-written data restored it as clean; the same settle was then added before every restore.
- A FIFO or a device in the data once went into a tar that its restore refused (review): the tars take only files, directories and links. A hard link to a link, and hard links across entries, made exports the import refused, so hard links are copied as files of their own (`--hard-dereference`), as the mirror's rsync without `-H` makes them anyway.
- Holes were written as zeros once: a copy took more disk than its source, past what `du` had measured (review), hence `--sparse` in the tars and in `RsyncCopy` for the mirror and syncs.
- Writing the tars through the container's stdout ran at 12 MB/s on a small DigitalOcean Droplet, 90 s of downtime for 1 GiB, where the same tar written to a file mounted into the container took a tenth of the time (measured 2026-10-26): hence `tarInto`.
- Decided 2026-10-26, no mirror by default: a mirror is a full second copy kept for good and saves downtime only on data that stays put between passes; a busy database's written tables change within minutes, and rsync copies a changed file whole between local directories, so the stopped pass is close to a full copy anyway. Reflinks are the answer for a large database.
- Reflinks first came with the clone helpers shared with syncs (`clone.go`); reflinked copies kept for good were rejected because a database writing over them fragments its files (measured on XFS: about two extents per 16 KiB page written). A server's first take had no util image, so the reflink check failed into a take without reflinks (e2e): the image is readied before the check.
- Hard links for the mirror were rejected over rsync passes: they give nothing for a database and cost compression, removal time and honest sizes.
- The files alone could not tell a `.tmp` cut before its rename from one cut before its tars, since a mirror take writes its tars after its manifest (review): `devopsy-tarred` marks a take without a mirror whose tars are all there.
- The sync lock was fd 5 in the snapshot scripts and fd 8 in a push's and an export's `syncSourceLock`, the image lock's fd everywhere else, until 2026-11-01: neither of those opens fd 5 otherwise, so it holds the sync lock on fd 5 too.
- Round 3: the first pass held no release lock, and a release switching current meanwhile left the stopped pass on the old release's labels with the new one's code; every step now runs in the release current when it starts.
- Review of 2026-10-25: a take's prune removed the snapshot a restore was reading, so a restore holds the snapshot lock from before it reads the snapshot and pruning keeps the one a restore's mark names.
- `--snapshots` listed a release snapshot INCOMPLETE while its detached tar still ran (review): the holding file is written once the take has its id, and the detached tar keeps the lock to its end.
- What a take stops grew in reviews: first the services with data, then any service with a writable mount of a copied entry or any mount of a copied volume (a worker writing the app's entry, review), and for a restore any service of the current release mounting what it writes (round 2); dependents follow as everywhere devopsy stops something.
- Round 3: a sync's push on the parent removed the record whole with its restore, dropping the `snapshot` lines of a take gone whole; the settle (`settleStops`) now starts them and runs before a push or an export on a target stops anything. A release dropping a service, killed before its `release-record live`, then failed every settle after it (review): a settle starts only what the current release has.
- The watcher signals the script alone where it leads no group, so a take's steps run in the background with the shell forwarding the signal: otherwise a cancel during the stopped pass left the services down.
- A full restore once carried the snapshot's marks into the new release (round 2): `make-release` takes only the pending file and `devopsy-target.env` of the release's files.
- Decompressing a tar through the container's stdout for the check ran at the same 8 MB/s as the data stream, 114 s for 1 GiB: `unzstd` decompresses into a FIFO beside the snapshots. The reader holds a writer of its own so it never waits on a container that never opened the FIFO, and a check killed outright left a container blocked opening it, removed first by the next check.
- Decided 2026-10-26: the space check of a restore is a warning, never a refusal, since sparse files counting whole make the estimate high; disk space on servers as a whole is a concern of its own, not the restore's.
- An app's absolute links (`storage/latest -> /app/storage/f`) made every snapshot unrestorable when the restore applied the import's rule on link targets (review): a symbolic link's target is the data's own wherever it leads, since nothing is written through a link and an extraction mounts its entry alone.
- Round 2: an imported snapshot's `storage/public -> /` was a bind source of the code and mounted the host's root after the restore; round 3: refusing every link under a bind source made `storage/public -> v1` backups unrestorable. The check now walks the tar's own links as the host resolves them in mnt (`linkAtBind`, `tarLinks.walkMnt`), refusing only a link that leads a bind source out of its entry; every release and rollback refuses such a bind too (`LeavingBinds`), since the data outlives the code it was checked in.
- Review of 2026-10-25: an imported manifest is anyone's, and a crafted one named another project's volume, which the restore emptied as root; the manifest's names must be single names, and a volume is restored by the name compose gives its key in the restored code.
- Round 2 of 2026-10-25: a restore on a marked environment wrote only the entries the snapshot had and cleared the mark, leaving an entry the snapshot lacked half-written; a restore refuses when any entry or volume of a data service, current or restored, is absent from the snapshot, as a sync must copy all of each to clear a mark.
- A data image pulled for a restore was stripped by another environment's removal before the data step held it (review): the pull runs under the image lock, shared, and bookmarks it as the snapshot's at once.
- Round 2: the data was written before `followOutput`'s marker, so a failure there read as one before the switch while the images' names had moved; `restore-data` now comes past the point of no return, right after the marker.
- Review of 2026-10-25: the data step cleared the mark, so a shell killed before the run step left the set down unmarked, and a restore run again left the cut one's stop lines; the mark and the record go only once the run step went through (`restored`).
- Round 2: the restart after a failed restore run step ran the failed step's services on the restored data; the restart now skips a marked environment, and the run step's `firstDown` takes down what it started first.
- Round 3: a `--no-code` restore whose run step failed left what it started running; it takes it down as a full restore's does.
- Review of 2026-10-25: `check-fit` once accepted a current data service the snapshot lacked on a marked environment, which the restore then cleared; it refuses one now, and any of its entries and volumes.
- A sync's copy mark written over a restore's mark let the next plain sync clear it (review): the mark stays the restore's, and a sync clears it only by copying every service of the snapshot, checked per service with `copiesService` from the child's `!restore` lines.
- Takes and releases with release snapshots waited 10 minutes for an export's lock, then failed (review): an export holds only its snapshot's directory, shared on fd 4, and removals skip a locked snapshot. A release waiting in vain for the sync or snapshot lock once exited 75 and stayed pending; it fails through `not_live` now (`lockWaitOr`).
- Round 2 of the import: GNU tar took a stream cut at a member's boundary for complete, so the same stream is read by `archive/tar` to its end-of-archive blocks. The import's umask 077 stripped the code's modes, which a restore then ran (review): `-p`.
- Round 3: a link among an imported snapshot's own files had the import read and write another snapshot's manifest, so nothing but files and directories is accepted outside `code/`; a crafted image listing had the server pull and tag another repository's image, and its removal strip names of images nothing else held (`images.FilterImported`).
- Round 4: a snapshot moved from another environment named its images after its own compose project, and its full restore found a listing of nothing; the import keeps each service's images under this environment's repository.
- Round 5: an imported listing's image ids were the other server's; bookmarked by them, a local image of another repository was tagged into the service's, which the containerd store then resolved by digest; ids are cleared, and a restore's pull counts an image there only when its own repository has it by digest.
- An imported release snapshot was pruned as one whose release had gone (review): it is made a manual one.
- Images stay out of exports because a member of the outer tar needs its size first, so `docker save` would spool gigabytes on the server; the README says to import only snapshots one trusts, since a full restore runs the snapshot's code.
- Review of 2026-10-25: a changed `snapshots: root` leaves the old snapshots unseen; finding them would need a sweep of Docker's bookmarks, which removals never do, so the README says to move them first (accepted).
- Release snapshots: one snapshot per release was rejected after a review found it removing the one before a failed run step, the only copy of the data from before it; every release snapshot counts towards `keep`, and a release tried again keeps its own.
- The bookmarks and the detached pruning hold the image lock, so `releaseSnapshotSetup` exported the server's image lock, record and mode (review); since 2026-11-02 the server's devopsy finds all three itself (`serverImages`, internals/images.md), and nothing is exported for them. A detached tar killed outright left a `.tmp` only a take settled (review): `release-check` runs the leftovers first.
- Every failure before the switch, the write after the fetch included, once left the record's `snapshot` lines down (review): they go through `not_live`, whose hook settles them.
- Decided: only a holder of the snapshot lock prunes; an activation calling a snapshot step would make every release need a server with snapshots.
- A cut sync's copy or a failed restore once had `release-record done` start the lines it should keep down (review): the record says so now. Round 4: a service left exited by the run step whose container another image held was started on the old image; `startLeftDown` makes it again (`images.Recreate`). A start that fails there once failed the release, live already (review): it only warns.
- A restore's mark that could not be written left the stopped services the snapshot's, which nothing started (review): the hook starts them.
- The gate: refusing a release with release snapshots on only once its activation starts, after the upload, is accepted, since an activation's needs are checked by its own session and the upload's by the upload's.
- The e2e server's home is a tmpfs where nothing clones, so the test of reflinks mounts an XFS loop device of its own where the project's directory goes (`TestSnapshotReflinkE2E`).
- What `startLeftDown` could not start stays in the record (decided 2026-11-04): it only warned, then `release-record done` and `snapshotRestored` removed the lines anyway, so with a run step that does not up every service nothing ever started those services. Found by cutting every docker call of an activation (`TestActivationCuts`). Not fixed then: a restore's failure past its switch takes the containers down, so a run step that does not up every service leaves those absent after the restore run again, which `startLeftDown` cannot `start`.
